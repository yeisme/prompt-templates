# vertical-short-drama-v1

- Readiness: first-support target; this does not mean production-ready.
- Common contract: 60–90 seconds, 12–18 shots, and 9:16; valid typed direction takes precedence.
- Opening: the first shot or first three seconds must show pressure, anomaly, an approaching objective, or an irreversible action.
- State change: every shot advances information, action, relationship, or risk; merge or remove purely decorative coverage.
- Dialogue: prioritize readable faces, speaker action, and listener reaction; avoid mechanical shot/reverse-shot repetition.
- Composition: favor faces, hand actions, vertical character layering, foreground/background occlusion, and one-handed-screen readability; avoid horizontal information overload.
- Movement budget: each scene normally has no more than two conspicuous moving-camera shots, reserved for revelation, approach, imbalance, or power change.
- Ending: the final one or two shots create a decision, reveal, danger, or unanswered question; do not substitute a generic freeze frame for a hook.
- Image handoff: keyframes identify subject version, action phase, vertical layers, light source, prop hand, and continuity notes.
- Review: total duration, shot density, dialogue readability, reusable locations, opening pressure, movement budget, and ending hook.
