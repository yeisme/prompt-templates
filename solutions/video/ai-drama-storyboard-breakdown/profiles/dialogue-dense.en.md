# dialogue-dense-v1

- Readiness: exploratory.
- Common contract: 60–120 seconds and 10–20 shots; valid typed direction takes precedence.
- Core job: cover every dialogue segment while showing speaker, listener reaction, pauses, avoidance, interruption, and power shifts.
- Shot strategy: avoid mechanical shot/reverse-shot repetition; framing changes must correspond to subtext, information advantage, relationship turns, or suppressed action.
- Reaction shots: use them only when the listener's strategy, judgment, or action changes; do not cut every line into an isolated reaction.
- Space: favor two-shots, foreground/background layers, and occlusion to express distance and power; crossing the axis requires a dramatic reason.
- Duration: allocate extra time only when silence or action carries information; never repeat dialogue coverage to fill the target duration.
- Content boundary: do not add, rewrite, or splice literal dialogue; a summary may describe dramatic function only.
- Image handoff: keyframes identify speaking/listening state, eyeline target, spatial relationship, and prohibited new lip shapes or dialogue.
- Review: dialogue coverage, speaker and reactor, axis, pauses, power changes, unmapped dialogue, and redundant coverage.
