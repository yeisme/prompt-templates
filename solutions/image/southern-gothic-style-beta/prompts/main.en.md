# Southern Gothic Style Template

A reusable style layer for Southern Gothic visuals: the decayed grandeur, religion, violence, repression and grotesque beauty of the American South — elegant and rotten at once. It is not European Gothic: no castles, no vampires; the dread comes from family, society and human nature itself, not from supernatural monsters. Only the setting, the subjects and the optional theme clause are swapped per image; every style paragraph stays fixed. Bind all variables before rendering; the rendered result is the final prompt body without further rewriting.

Southern Gothic atmosphere, elegant decay: the faded glory of the old South sinking into humid rot; a once-magnificent world of decaying mansions, failing farms, small-town churches, graveyards, abandoned cars, faded wallpaper and broken furniture; the scene looks calm and still, yet danger, secrets and a sense of death seep through everything.

Nature lush to the point of corruption: Spanish moss hanging from dead trees, creeping vines swallowing wooden walls, swamps and wetlands, cotton fields, dense Southern woods, insects, crows, deer bones and wildflowers; humid summer haze over rural isolation, small towns, bayous, Victorian farmhouses and clapboard cabins.

Grotesque elegance in the figures: recluses, preachers, widows, old Southern families, drifters, odd characters, children and marginalized people bearing psychological wounds and moral contradictions; dignity and deformity side by side, solemn and unsettling, never glamorized and never mocked.

Muted earthy palette: faded brown, gray-green, dirty white, dark red, tobacco yellow, mud tones; the look of an old photograph or sun-bleached film, colors drained by heat and time.

Oppressive Southern light: muggy blazing sunlight, dim interiors, candlelight, dusk, a heavy overcast sky before a storm; strong chiaroscuro contrasts, hot exterior glare against shadowed rooms, light that reveals decay rather than flattering it.

Textures of time and rot: old wood, rusted iron, dust, sweat, mud, cracked paint, mold stains, lace, worn Bibles and antiques; everything bears the erosion of years, tactile and lived-in, never clean, never new.
